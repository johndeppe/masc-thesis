Maintaining TLB coherence is required for correctness but presents system overheads that impede multi-core scaling. One approach to reduce TLB coherence  overhead is to reduce TLB shootdown costs by sending shootdown messages to only those TLBs that contained the now-invalid page mapping. We investigate the costs of sharer lists for the purpose of shootdown filtering and propose methods for their use.

We began this project hoping to make the TLB maintenance process simpler for the systems programmer, as it is described as a substantial difficulty by new OS writers \textbackslash{}cite\{boos\_theseus\_2022\} and (how do I cite Email with Boos and talking to Twizzler author Daniel Bittman in Margo's CPSC 509? I need to find the date probably.) BypassD-style systems \textbackslash{}cite\{yadalam\_bypassd\_2024\} make this even more important if filesystems become integrated into the translation process.

However, we have failed, and instead have added yet another knob to Linux memory management.
